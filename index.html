The Long Frieze

An interactive timeline through five thousand years of art made on the Indian subcontinent — from a bronze figure buried in Mohenjo-daro to the canvases of independent India. Select a period to open its artifacts.

Each period gathers objects in stone, bronze, paint, and architecture that survive from it, with notes on where each piece was made, who made or commissioned it, and where it can be seen today. The aim is not a complete catalogue but a working sample — enough to trace how materials, patronage, and technique changed from the Bronze Age river cities of the Indus to the ateliers and academies of the twentieth century.

Click any period on the timeline above to open its artifacts. Periods with more than one artifact show small tabs so you can switch between them without leaving the page. Every period also has its own shareable link — try adding #mughal to this page's URL.

This timeline covers seven periods and fourteen objects as a working sample of Indian art history, not an exhaustive catalogue — entire traditions, including much of South Indian temple painting, Rajasthani and Pahari miniature schools, and contemporary photography and installation art, sit outside its current scope and would be natural directions to extend it.

Artifact photographs are sourced from Wikimedia Commons and are either in the public domain or released under Creative Commons licences; individual credits appear beneath each image. Historical and biographical details are drawn from standard museum and scholarly references and simplified for a general audience.
